What the width does to the choice. B1 was my recommendation on paper. In the real cell it cuts at “…overridden in” and the count — the thing worth reading — is the part that disappears. A truncated number is worse than no number.

B2 says less but survives. “Reaches 2 of 3 projects” is literally true and fits with room to spare. Its cost: it never says why the third differs, so a reader may read it as a failure rather than a local choice.

B4 is new, from seeing this. Dropping the noun after the first count buys nine characters and keeps both numbers. It leans on the column header “Beyond the store” to supply the context. Terser than anything else in the cell, which may be a step too far.
